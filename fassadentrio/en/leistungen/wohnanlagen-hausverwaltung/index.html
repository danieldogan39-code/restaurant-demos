Facade cleaning for residential complexes, property managers and owners' associations

We clean residential complexes of any size, reliably and on schedule. The free test patch gives owners and advisory boards a basis for their decision.

Request a free test patchHow the cleaning works

A decision that many people share

In an owners' association, work on the facade is usually decided by the owners' meeting. That takes more than a quote on paper. We therefore clean a test patch on the property in advance, free of charge. Owners and the advisory board see the result with their own eyes and can compare it with repainting.

Advice, planning and execution from a single source

We accompany the project from the initial consultation through planning to professional execution and carry out every step ourselves. Because we work with telescopic lances and an aerial work platform, no scaffolder has to be hired.

Economical for the community

Cleaning costs up to 70% less than repainting and postpones it by many years. The long-term protection delays regrowth for up to 5 years and can be renewed at any time.

What a property management company reports

On Google, a property management company describes working with FassadenTrio as smooth, right from the first inspection. You can read all reviews in the Google profile of FassadenTrio.

Frequently asked questions

Do you also clean several buildings in one complex?

Yes. We clean residential complexes of any size and also handle large properties, throughout Germany and Europe.

Is the test patch also available for large properties?

Yes. The test patch is free and can be requested at any time.

Does scaffolding have to be put up?

No. Our technicians work from the ground with telescopic lances up to 12 metres long. For taller buildings we use an aerial work platform. There are no scaffolding costs.

How long does the facade stay clean?

After cleaning we apply a transparent long-term protection. It delays regrowth for up to 5 years. The protection can be reapplied at any time.
